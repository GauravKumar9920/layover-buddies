Short on time · 5 to 6 hours

A 5 or 6-hour layover in Mumbai: worth leaving?

Sometimes yes, often no, and it’s better to know which before you land. Here’s the honest time maths, the places close enough to the airport to make a short window work, and the signs that you should stay in the terminal.

Updated September 2026· 5 min read· An honest answer beats a missed flight

A short layover is where the dream of “popping into the city” meets the reality of immigration queues and Mumbai traffic. The trick is to count the hours you actually have outside, not the hours printed on your ticket.

The time maths

Block of timeRough allowance
Landing to leaving arrivals (immigration, bags, cloakroom)45–90 minutes
Getting back and through departures for an international flightAround 3 hours before departure is the usual advice
Road time to a place near the airport, each way20–45 minutes

Add those up and a 5-hour layover can leave you with well under an hour outside, most of it in a car. A 6-hour layover gives you a little more room, but only for somewhere close. South Mumbai, with its 45–75 minute drive each way, is realistically off the table for both. Your airline’s check-in rules and your own comfort with tight timings should always win. Five hours vs six

Five hours: for most travellers, stay airside. Eat well, rest, and save the city for a longer stop. Leaving is only worth it if immigration is quick, you have no checked bags to deal with, and you’re happy to turn back the moment the clock says so.

Six hours: a short outing near the airport can work if your inbound flight lands on time. Pick one place, keep your phone charged, and set your “leave now” time before you go.

Close to the airport

These are the places a short window can reach. All of them are in the suburbs around the airport, so you avoid the long drive south.

  • Juhu Beach: a wide stretch of sand where Mumbai comes to walk, snack and watch the sea. Good for a quick breath of sea air and street food from the stalls along the beach.
  • Bandra waterfront: Bandstand promenade and Bandra Fort (or the whole Bandra walk), with a view of the Bandra–Worli Sea Link from the shore. Mount Mary Basilica is just up the hill, and cafés are a short walk into the lanes.
  • East of the airport: Powai Lake and the 2,000-year-old Mahakali Caves are among the shortest drives of all from Terminal 2.
  • A café stop in Bandra or Juhu: sometimes the best short layover is simply a proper meal and a cutting chai somewhere that isn’t a terminal.

With eight hours or more, the whole city opens up. See the 8-hour plan.

When to stay put

  • Your inbound flight is running late.
  • You need a visa and don’t have one yet.
  • You’re leaving during evening rush hour or heavy monsoon rain. See our monsoon guide.
  • Your onward flight leaves from a different terminal and you’re already short on time.

If a short window is too tight, we’ll say so. That’s part of planning a detour honestly.

Quick answers

Is 5 hours enough to leave Mumbai airport?
Usually not. After immigration and the time you need to get back through departures for an international flight, a 5-hour layover can leave well under an hour outside, much of it in a car. For most travellers, staying at the airport is the better call.
What can I do near Mumbai airport on a 6-hour layover?
Stay close. Juhu Beach and the Bandra waterfront, with its view of the Bandra–Worli Sea Link, are in the western suburbs near the airport. South Mumbai is usually too far for a 6-hour window.
Do I need a visa for a short layover in Mumbai?
You only need a visa if you leave the airport. To step outside you must clear Indian immigration, which requires a valid visa, however short your stay.
