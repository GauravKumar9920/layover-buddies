Practical · Respect and comfort

What to wear, how to be.

Mumbai is relaxed and cosmopolitan, and people are kind to visitors who make a little effort. A few habits, and one light layer in your bag, cover almost everything.

Updated September 2026· 4 min read· Cool, covered, curious

You don’t need a special wardrobe for a Mumbai layover. You need to stay cool, be comfortable walking, and be ready to cover up for a temple, mosque or church.

What to wear

  • Light, loose, breathable clothes. Cotton and linen beat anything tight. It’s warm and humid for most of the year.
  • Shoulders and knees covered works everywhere, from a museum to a mosque, and is simply cooler in the sun.
  • A scarf or light layer in your bag covers your head or shoulders when needed, and fights air-conditioning on the plane.
  • Shoes you can slip off. You’ll remove them at most places of worship.

Visiting temples, mosques and churches

Many of the places in our place library are living places of worship. They welcome respectful visitors, and a few habits go a long way:

  • Follow the customs posted at the entrance; they differ from place to place.
  • Expect to take off your shoes. There’s usually a place to leave them.
  • Some places ask visitors to cover their heads, or have separate areas for men and women.
  • Speak softly, and don’t walk in front of people who are praying.
  • Photography may be restricted inside. When in doubt, don’t.

Our field notes for Haji Ali, Siddhivinayak and Mount Mary include what to expect.

Photography

Mumbai is endlessly photogenic, and its people are not scenery. Ask before photographing someone, especially at work, at prayer or with children. A smile and a gesture at your camera is usually enough; if someone says no, move on. Avoid photographing security installations, including inside the airport.

Everyday manners

  • Use your right hand to give and receive money or food.
  • Public displays of affection beyond holding hands can draw attention.
  • Bargaining is normal at street stalls and markets, with a smile; it isn’t done in shops with fixed prices.

Mumbai speaks Marathi, Hindi, English and many other languages. English will get you a long way.

Quick answers

What should I wear in Mumbai?
Light, loose clothes in cotton or linen, with shoulders and knees covered. Carry a scarf or light layer for places of worship and the plane, and wear shoes you can slip off.
Do I need to take off my shoes at temples in Mumbai?
Usually, yes. Most temples, mosques and many shrines ask visitors to remove their shoes, and some ask you to cover your head. Follow the customs posted at the entrance.
Can I take photos in Mumbai?
Yes, but ask before photographing people, especially at work or prayer. Photography may be restricted inside places of worship and around security areas.
