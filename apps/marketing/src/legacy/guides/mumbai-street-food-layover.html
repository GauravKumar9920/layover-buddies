Mumbai street food on a layover

For a lot of travellers, the best hour of a Mumbai layover is the one spent eating. Here’s what to try, where it tastes best, what it costs, and how to enjoy it without worrying about the long flight ahead.

Updated September 2026· 6 min read· A short list of big flavours

Mumbai eats on the move. Office workers, students and families share the same stalls, and many of the city’s most famous dishes cost less than a coffee back home. On a layover, that’s perfect: big flavour, little time.

What to eat

DishWhat it isRough price
Vada pavA spiced potato fritter in a soft bun with chutneys. Mumbai’s own fast food.≈ ₹20

Prices vary by neighbourhood and how smart the place is. Treat them as a feel rather than a quote.

Where to find it

  • The seafront: bhel and snacks at Girgaon Chowpatty, at the northern end of Marine Drive, and pav bhaji at the stalls along Juhu Beach.
  • Fort and Colaba: old Irani cafés for bun maska and chai, perfect between the heritage streets of Fort and Kala Ghoda.
  • Near the office districts: busy lunchtime stalls serving vada pav and quick plates to the city’s workers.

Eat well, fly well

You have a flight to catch, so be a little choosier than you would on a long trip:

  • Pick busy stalls. A queue of locals means fresh food and fast turnover.
  • Choose hot and freshly cooked. Food made in front of you is the safest bet.
  • Drink sealed bottled water. Skip tap water and ice if you’re unsure where it came from.
  • Go easy on spice before a long flight. Ask for it kam teekha, less spicy.
  • Carry small notes and coins. Many stalls take UPI, but visitors may not be able to use it, so cash is simplest.

Quick answers

What street food should I try in Mumbai on a layover?
Start with vada pav (about ₹20), pav bhaji, bhel puri and a cutting chai. If you have a little longer, add bun maska and Irani chai at an old café, or a falooda for dessert.
Is Mumbai street food safe for travellers?
Plenty of visitors enjoy it without trouble. Choose busy stalls where food is cooked fresh in front of you, drink sealed bottled water, skip ice you’re unsure about, and go easy on spice before a long flight.
How much does street food cost in Mumbai?
Very little. A vada pav is about ₹20 and a cutting chai about ₹15–₹20. A filling street-food lunch can cost a few hundred rupees.
