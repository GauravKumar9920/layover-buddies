# Detour — Website Review: Making It Feel Human

**Date:** 10 June 2026 · **Page reviewed:** detourtrips.com (live)

Your instinct is right: the bones are excellent — the design is distinctive, the structure is logical, and the "seven contradictions" concept is genuinely good. But the copy reads like it was written by a very good machine: polished, rhythmic, and weirdly frictionless. Real founders writing about their own city leave fingerprints. Right now there are none. Here's exactly where the AI smell comes from and how to fix it.

---

## 1. Why it feels like AI (the specific tells)

**a) The "Not X. Y." construction appears three times.**
"Not packages — possibilities." · "Not stock photos." · "Not the generic postcard tourist trap." This is the single most recognizable LLM tic in 2026. One is fine. Three is a pattern.

**b) Every section heading uses the same italic-word trick.**
*detour*, *fall a little in love*, *local student*, *seven contradictions*, *terminal to the city*, *live it*, *always ask*, *all of it*. The device is nice — but when it's mechanical (every single heading), it reads as templated, not designed.

**c) The seven contradiction cards are too perfectly parallel.**
"Sea & skyline. Chaos & calm. Old & new. Street & lounges. Gothic & docks. Grit & gold. Stars & shrines." Every card: two nouns, an ampersand, then one sentence of stacked adjectives ("adrenaline-fueled, packed local trains", "relentless, sweat-stained hustle", "opulent, quiet mansions"). Humans break their own patterns. Keep the structure but let two or three cards breathe differently — one could be a single short memory instead of a description.

**d) Repeated phrases betray generation.**
"A city that suddenly feels like home" (hero) vs. "a city that suddenly feels like it's on your side" (next section) — the same sentence, regenerated. "Verified local student" appears six times. Vary or cut.

**e) There are no humans on a site about humans.**
This is the deepest problem, bigger than any phrasing:
- No founder. Who made this? Why? Nobody signs anything.
- No real guides. "Arjun, 4.92★, replies in 6 min" is a mockup — and you have zero completed trips. A fabricated rating on a pre-launch product is the kind of thing a traveler (or journalist) will notice, and it costs you the exact trust you're selling.
- No testimonials, no faces, no names, no story of a single real detour.

**f) Zero concrete numbers from lived experience.**
AI writes "honest local prices." A Mumbaikar writes "a vada pav is ₹20, the Sea Link toll is ₹100, and chai at Kyani costs less than your airport water bottle — verify these numbers yourself before publishing; the point is that *you* know them and AI doesn't." Specificity is the cheapest authenticity there is.

## 2. Concrete rewrites (before → after)

**Hero subhead**
> Before: "A layover used to be dead time between flights. Detour opens the gates: a custom route matching your hours, a verified student buddy, and a city that suddenly feels like home."
>
> After: "You've got eight hours in Mumbai and no idea what's possible. We match you with a local college student who does — they plan a route around your flight, meet you at arrivals, and get you back with time to spare."

**The Arjun mock card** — keep the UI, change the label. Add a small tag: "What your match will look like" — or better, replace with a real guide once you have permission. Change "4.92 ★" to "VJTI, 3rd year" or the guide's actual college. College name is a real credential; an invented rating is a liability.

**"Not packages — possibilities."**
> After: "These aren't fixed packages. They're three examples of the kind of day your buddy might build, depending on whether you're a food person, a history person, or just need sea air after 14 hours in a middle seat."

**Transparent pricing card**
> Before: "Cabs, food, and tickets are paid directly at the real local price."
>
> After: "You pay for cabs, food and tickets directly, at the same price a local pays — a vada pav is ₹20 (about 25 cents), and your buddy will tell you when something's overpriced before you reach for your wallet."

**Add a founder note (new section, ~80 words, before the FAQ).** This is the single highest-impact humanizing change:
> "I'm Gaurav. I started Detour because I kept meeting travelers who spent 14 hours inside BOM, twenty minutes from one of the most alive cities on earth, because leaving felt too risky alone. The first detours are free — honestly, because we want you to come back home raving about Mumbai more than we want your money right now. Email me directly: admin@detourtrips.com."
>
> *(Adjust the wording to actually sound like you — write it yourself, badly, in one draft, and resist polishing it. That's the point.)*

**FAQ — add the two missing questions that actually decide bookings:**
1. **"Do I need a visa to leave the airport?"** Yes — most nationalities need an Indian e-visa (~$25, apply online before flying; allow 72h processing). Without this answer on the page, every reader hits a wall the moment they Google it themselves.
2. **"I'm a solo female traveler — is this for me?"** Answer directly: buddy verification, route transparency, share-your-live-location, option to request a female guide if available. Don't bury it.

## 3. Conversion fixes (channeling visitors from abroad)

1. **Kill the mailto CTA.** "Request a Detour" opens an email client — which does nothing for the majority of people on phones/Gmail-in-browser. Replace with a free **Tally.so form** (arrival/departure/flight no./interests) embedded in your existing modal. You'll also stop losing the people who close the email half-written.
2. **Add WhatsApp.** Your audience lives on it, and it signals "real humans." Free WhatsApp Business app + a `wa.me` link as a secondary CTA: "WhatsApp us your flight times."
3. **Trade on newness instead of hiding it.** "We're matching our very first travelers" is good — go further: **"Founding traveler" framing.** First 25 travelers get the trip free + a named thank-you on the site. Scarcity + honesty beats simulated social proof.
4. **Capture the not-ready-yet majority.** Most visitors have a layover *someday*, not next week. Add a one-field email capture: "Flying through Mumbai later this year? Leave your email — we'll send you the 1-page layover cheat sheet." (Free Brevo/Mailchimp tier; the cheat sheet doubles as content marketing.)
5. **Show real photos as soon as you have one real detour.** Even one real traveler + buddy photo at Marine Drive outranks every stock-style gallery image on this page for trust.
6. **Currency/cultural anchors for foreigners:** show prices in ₹ *and* $/€ once, mention UPI/cash reality, and "your buddy speaks English" explicitly above the fold (it's currently buried in a card).

---

## 4. What NOT to change

- The design system, paper texture, type pairing — distinctive, keep.
- The "seven contradictions" concept — just roughen the execution.
- "How we travel" (dignity/safety/honesty) — best section on the page; it already sounds like a held belief rather than copy. Notably, it's the section with the fewest AI tells. Match the rest of the site to it.
- Free-in-early-access positioning — clear and disarming.

---

## Priority order

1. Founder note + remove/relabel fake rating *(trust — do first, 1 hour)*
2. Tally form + WhatsApp CTA *(conversion, 1–2 hours)*
3. Visa + solo-female FAQ entries *(conversion + SEO, 1 hour)*
4. De-pattern the copy per rewrites above *(voice, 2–3 hours)*
5. Email capture for future flyers *(pipeline, 1 hour)*
