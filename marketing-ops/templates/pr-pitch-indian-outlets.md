# PR Pitch — Indian Outlets (First Wave)

**Targets:** YourStory, Mid-Day, Hindustan Times Mumbai, Condé Nast Traveller India, The Better India, LBB Mumbai, and any Mumbai city-desk or startup reporter you can find a named contact for.

**Angle:** "Mumbai students turn airport layovers into friendships" — a human, local, founder-led story, not a product launch. Lead with the people and the city, not the app.

**Send from:** admin@detourtrips.com · **Best format:** short, personal email. No attachments in the first email (offer them).

---

## Subject lines (pick/test one)
- A Mumbai student showed a stranger the city between two flights
- The startup turning BOM layovers into a reason to love Mumbai
- "Don't waste your layover in the terminal" — a Mumbai founder's pitch

---

## Email body

Hi [First name],

I read your piece on [specific recent article — name it] and thought this might be your kind of story.

I'm Gaurav, founder of Detour (detourtrips.com). We match international travellers stuck on long Mumbai layovers with verified local college students who plan a route around their flight, meet them at arrivals, and get them back with time to spare. It's free while we're in early access — right now we care more about travellers flying home raving about Mumbai than about revenue.

The story I think is worth telling isn't the app — it's the moment: a 22-year-old from [college] walking a stranger from [country] through Dadar's food stalls, and both of them keeping in touch after. Twelve hours that would've been spent in a transit lounge become the reason someone falls for this city.

We're matching our first "founding travellers" over the next few weeks. I'd love to give you first access to the story — including the students themselves, who are far more interesting than I am.

Worth a quick call, or shall I send a short backgrounder?

Thanks for reading,
Gaurav
Founder, Detour · Mumbai
admin@detourtrips.com · [phone] · instagram.com/detour_trips

## Notes
- Keep the first email under ~180 words. Journalists skim.
- Offer the *students* as the story. Founders pitching themselves is noise; a 3rd-year engineering student showing Mumbai to a stranger is a feature.
- If they bite, have ready: 2–3 founding-traveler photos (with consent), one student willing to be interviewed, and the honest "free in early access" framing.
