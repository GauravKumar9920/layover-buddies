**Prepared:** April 2026
**Posture:** Ultra-lean bootstrapping
**Scope:** India-based two-sided marketplace connecting international layover travelers with Mumbai college students as local guides. Foreign-currency inbound revenue, INR payouts to guides. Not yet incorporated.

Before signing contracts or committing capital, get a one-time paid consult with an Indian startup/tourism lawyer — budget ₹15–25K for a scoping session. ## 1. Executive Summary — What You Actually Need To Do

India's regulatory environment is **surprisingly favorable** for this model. Tourism licensing is largely voluntary, tourist-guide licensing only bites inside ASI-protected monuments, and the "technology intermediary" shield is well-established in Indian law. The real operational risks are not tourism-specific — they're the **standard marketplace risks** (payments, tax, gig-worker classification, consumer protection, data protection).

The short version of the plan:

1. **Incorporate as Private Limited** via Razorpay Rize (₹5K government fees) — preserves fundraising optionality.
2. **Get DPIIT Startup India recognition** (free, 7 days) — unlocks tax benefits and IPR rebates.
3. **Use Razorpay or Cashfree as your payment aggregator** — both now hold full PA-CB licenses, so you don't need your own cross-border license.
4. **Register as a Tour Operator under GST (HSN 9985) at 5% without ITC** — don't try to claim export-of-services zero-rating; the Indian GST system treats inbound tours as taxable in India.
5. **Position everything as "connecting travelers with independent local buddies"** — not "our tours" — to preserve intermediary status and sidestep guide-licensing exposure.
6. **18+ Indian citizens only as guides** (hard gate), with PAN, Aadhaar verification, and police background check.
7. **Layered ToS + Guide Contractor Agreement + Insurance stack** is your real liability shield — not any single clause.
8. **DPDP Act compliance has a 2027 runway** but start now; consent flow, grievance officer, privacy policy are low-cost Day 1 items.

**Estimated Year 1 legal/compliance cost:** ₹1.8–2.8 lakh (incorporation + registrations + lawyer fees for docs + insurance + CA retainer). This is the realistic floor for doing things properly.

## 2. Incorporation — Go Private Limited, Here's Why

| Dimension | Pvt Ltd | LLP | OPC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum members | 2 directors, 2 shareholders | 2 partners | 1 + nominee |
| Foreign investment | Yes (automatic route for most sectors) | Yes with conditions | **Not allowed** |
| ESOPs | Yes | **No** | Requires conversion |
| VC-fundable | Default | VCs won't invest | Must convert |
| Govt + professional incorporation cost | ₹5K–15K | ₹5K–10K | ₹7K–12K |
| Annual compliance cost | ₹15K–35K | ₹8K–15K | ₹12K–25K |
| Audit threshold | Mandatory Day 1 | Turnover > ₹40L or capital > ₹25L | Mandatory Day 1 |
| Tax rate | 22% (Sec 115BAA) or 25% (turnover < ₹400 Cr) | 30% flat + surcharge | 22% / 25% |

**Recommendation: Private Limited**, for four reasons:
1. VCs, angels, and accelerators only invest in Pvt Ltd.
2. ESOPs for co-founders and early hires.
3. Foreign investment allowed — relevant given your international user base and likelihood of overseas angel interest.
4. DPIIT recognition and 80-IAC tax holiday work cleanly.

The ~₹15–20K/year extra compliance vs LLP is trivial compared to the cost of forced conversion later (tax-messy and takes 3–6 months).

**Cheapest path:** [Razorpay Rize](https://razorpay.com/rize/) — **free** Pvt Ltd incorporation if you open a current account with Razorpay. You pay only the government fees (~₹4–6K including stamp duty in Maharashtra). This is genuinely the best ultra-lean option because you'll need a current account anyway and Razorpay's payment stack fits your use case perfectly.

**Alternative vendors** (if you want to keep payment provider separate from incorporator): IndiaFilings (₹7,899–12,999), Vakilsearch (₹6,999–11,999), ClearTax (₹9,999).

**Use the SPICe+ form on MCA portal** — bundles DIN, PAN, TAN, EPFO, ESIC, Professional Tax, and bank account opening into one filing. Timeline: 7–15 days.

**File Section 115BAA election with your first income tax return** to lock in the 22% concessional rate.

### DPIIT Recognition — What It Actually Gets You

1. **Section 80-IAC tax holiday** — 100% profit deduction for any **3 consecutive years** within first 10 years of incorporation. **[Finance Act 2025 extended eligibility to startups incorporated before March 31, 2030.](https://x.com/FinMinIndia/status/2013484292534608330)** You apply separately to the Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) after DPIIT recognition — approvals now take ~120 days. Historically a high bar; apply only after you have meaningful revenue/traction. Over 3,700 startups have been granted 80-IAC exemption since inception, with [187 approved in April 2025 alone](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2128860).

2. **Angel tax exemption under Section 56(2)(viib)** — Finance Act 2024 **abolished Section 56(2)(viib) entirely** from April 1, 2025, so this benefit is now moot for everyone. DPIIT status still useful for other shields.

3. **IPR fast-track** — 80% rebate on patent filing fees, 50% on trademark fees, free empanelled facilitators. Relevant when you trademark "Mumbai Buddies."

4. **Self-certification** under 9 labour laws and 3 environmental laws for 5 years.

5. **Public procurement exemption** from prior-turnover requirements on GeM.

**Do DPIIT recognition immediately after incorporation — it's free, fast, and unlocks IPR rebates plus self-certifications. Apply for 80-IAC later.**

## 4. Tourism Licensing — The Loophole Analysis

### Is a Tour Operator License Mandatory? **No.**

The Ministry of Tourism operates a **voluntary approval scheme** for Inbound Tour Operators (ITO), Tour Operators (TO), Travel Agents (TA), etc. MoT approval is a **quality-mark accreditation**, not a license to operate. You can run an inbound tour business without it.

Benefits of MoT approval (for reference, skip at launch):
- GST concessions on services to foreign tourists (some)
- Saathi app listing
- Eligibility for government tourism contracts
- Visa facilitation letters for clients

Requirements (not worth pursuing at launch):
- ~₹20 lakh minimum turnover
- 150 sq ft office space in commercial premises
- IATA/IITTM-qualified staff
- Proof of foreign exchange earnings via AD bank

**Recommendation:** Skip MoT approval at launch. Reconsider when you hit ~₹50L+ annual revenue and want the credibility signal.

### Is a Tourist Guide License Required? **Mostly no, with one exception.**

The Regional Level Guide (RLG) program run by **IITTM** trains and licenses guides for **ASI-protected monuments** (Taj Mahal, Elephanta Caves, etc.). Outside ASI premises, there is **no pan-India statutory requirement** for a license to accompany a paying tourist and share information.

- **Street walks, neighborhood tours, food tours, markets, non-ASI attractions:** No license required. Enforcement is non-existent.
- **ASI monuments (Elephanta Caves is the main Mumbai-adjacent one):** Only ASI/MoT-licensed guides can legally do paid guiding *inside* the protected premises. Enforced by ASI site staff via on-spot fines or eviction.
- **Maharashtra:** MTDC maintains a list of state-approved guides but does not have a statutory monopoly on city walking tours in Mumbai.

**Supreme Court position** (2015 ASI-related PILs): Upheld monument-specific restrictions; did **NOT** extend universal licensing to all guiding activity in India.

### The Language Loophole

The dominant marketplace playbook (Withlocals, ToursByLocals, Airbnb Experiences) uses language deliberately chosen to sidestep guide-licensing regimes:

- ❌ "Licensed Mumbai tour guides"
- ❌ "Our tours"
- ❌ "Professional guides"
- ✅ "Meet a local Buddy"
- ✅ "Connecting travelers with independent local companions"
- ✅ "Hosts" / "Locals" / "Buddies"
- ✅ "Experiences led by Mumbai residents"

**Apply this consistently across:**
- Landing page copy
- App onboarding
- Guide-facing messaging
- ToS
- Social media
- Press

Your CLAUDE.md already uses "Buddies" — keep it that way everywhere.

### Elephanta / ASI Handling

If a Buddy's itinerary includes Elephanta Caves (likely popular since it's accessible from CSMIA):

1. Buddy accompanies traveler as **companion/translator/logistics helper**, not guide.
2. Inside ASI premises, traveler hires an on-site licensed ASI guide (standard practice — ~₹500–1,500 fee, bookable at the entrance).
3. Your app can surface this as "local guide available on-site — not included; estimated ₹500 cash."

This preserves both compliance and traveler experience.

### CSMIA Airport Access

CSMIA is operated by Adani Airport Holdings. Commercial vehicle access and branded pickup require permits (expensive — lakhs per year). **Your Buddy should meet travelers curbside as an ordinary meeter-greeter** — no branded signage, no uniform, no counter. This is legally uncontroversial.

No known "tour operator partner program" at CSMIA as of 2025. Watch [csmia.adaniairports.com](https://csmia.adaniairports.com) for future opportunities — worth pitching once you have traction.

### CSMIA has no free transit tour program

Unlike Singapore Changi's famous free transit tours or Istanbul's TourIstanbul, Mumbai has **no airport-sponsored transit tour**. This is your market gap. The closest existing options are hotel concierges and prepaid taxi "3-hr sightseeing" packages (~₹2,500–4,000, inconsistent quality) and a few small operators on GetYourGuide/Viator with modest volume. **No student-guide marketplace exists in this space anywhere in India.**

## 5. Payments & Foreign Exchange — Use a Licensed PA-CB

### The Key Fact

As of April 2026, **~19 entities hold full RBI Payment Aggregator — Cross Border (PA-CB) licenses**, including:
- [Cashfree Payments](https://www.medianama.com/2024/07/223-cashfree-first-entity-rbi-cross-border-pa-license/) — first to get it, July 2024
- [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-rbi-cross-border-licence-global-payments/) — full license December 2, 2025
- Pine Labs, Easebuzz, PayU, PayPal, Amazon Pay India, Payoneer India, and others

**You do NOT need your own PA-CB license.** Use Razorpay or Cashfree as your licensed intermediary. They handle:
- Collection in foreign currency via international card networks
- Conversion to INR
- Settlement to your Indian current account
- Issue of FIRCs/FIRAs for tax/FEMA purposes
- Splitting payouts to guide sub-accounts (via Razorpay Route / Cashfree Easy Split)

### Cost Structure to Model Into Your Pricing

| Item | Rate |
|---|---|
| International card MDR | 3.0–3.5% + GST |
| AMEX international | up to 3.95% |
| Cross-border FX markup | 1–2% over RBI reference rate |
| Route/Split payouts | typically bundled, +0.25% |
| Settlement cycle | T+2 to T+5 |
| Chargeback fee | ₹1,500–2,500 per dispute |

**Model 4.0–4.5% all-in as your cost of accepting international cards.** This is a real margin hit vs domestic — factor into your commission %.

### Your FEMA Obligations (Even With PSP as PA-CB)

1. **AD Code registration** at your bank branch — one-time, free. Required to receive forex.
2. **FIRC / e-FIRA retention** — your PSP will pass through per-transaction advices. Keep for **8 years minimum** (FEMA + Income Tax).
3. **Purpose code tagging** — tour services typically tagged **P0802** ("Other tourist services"). Confirm at PSP onboarding.
4. **Realization window** — export proceeds must be realized within 9 months of invoice. PSP settlement in T+5 is fine.

### IEC + AD Code = Two Small Steps, Huge Unlock

- [Apply for IEC at DGFT](https://www.dgft.gov.in/CP/?opt=ieee-profile) — ₹500 govt fee, 1–3 days, PAN-based.
- Request AD Code from your bank RM after current account is opened — takes 1–2 weeks.

Without both, you cannot legally receive foreign inward remittances.

### EEFC Account (Nice-To-Have)

[Exchange Earners' Foreign Currency account](https://www.hdfcbank.com/personal/save/accounts/eefc-account) holds 100% of your forex receipts in USD/EUR/GBP indefinitely. Lets you pay overseas vendors (AWS, Vercel, Supabase, Apple Developer, Google Ads if billed USD) without round-trip conversion — saves ~1.5–2% each way.

Caveat: Your PSP typically settles in INR, so EEFC only helps if you can negotiate USD settlement or receive direct wire transfers.

**Recommendation:** Open it when you set up your current account; use it opportunistically.

## 6. GST Reality Check — You Are a Tour Operator, Not an Exporter

### The Hard Truth

**Inbound tours to foreign travelers are NOT "export of services" under GST, despite earning forex.**

The IGST Act's definition of export requires all five of:
1. Supplier in India
2. Recipient outside India
3. Place of supply outside India
4. Payment in convertible forex
5. Supplier/recipient not establishments of distinct persons

**The trap is #3.** Section 13(3)(b) IGST Act states that services requiring the physical presence of the recipient (like tours) → place of supply = where services are performed = Mumbai, India. So POS is India, not export, not zero-rated.

This has been upheld consistently:
- **Cozmo Travel World Pvt Ltd** (Kerala AAR, 2019): inbound tours NOT exports.
- **Crown Tours & Travels** (Rajasthan AAR, 2020): similar.
- **Tui India / Cox & Kings lines of cases**: same.

The only specific carve-out for tour operators is a narrow concession for **outbound** tours sold to foreigners visiting a foreign country — not your use case.

### Two Paths

**Path A (Year 1, if staying lean):** Stay under ₹20 lakh aggregate turnover → no GST registration required. Realistic if you're testing product-market fit with ≤1,000 tours.

**Path B (Year 2+, standard):** Register as Tour Operator under HSN 9985 with one of two GST rate elections:
- **5% without ITC** ← industry standard; simpler
- **18% with ITC** ← only beneficial if your input costs (platform infrastructure, marketing, vendor payments) have meaningful GST you can reclaim

At your stage, **5% without ITC** is almost certainly the right choice.

> Important nuance on GST registration: Even under ₹20L, Section 24 CGST technically mandates registration for inter-state/export supply. But Notification 10/2017-IT exempts sub-₹20L service providers from this. So you're genuinely free of GST registration under ₹20L turnover — confirm in writing with your CA before relying on this.

### Commission Model vs Principal Model

Think hard about how to invoice:

- **Principal model** (you're the tour operator, guides are subcontractors): 5% GST on full tour price collected from traveler. Guide raises invoice to you for their share; if guide is unregistered, no GST on their side, but reverse-charge may apply on certain services.
- **Commission model** (you're an agent, guide is the principal): 18% GST on your commission only; guide's price flows through. GST becomes the guide's problem — but most students won't be GST-registered, which creates practical issues.
- **Marketplace / e-commerce operator model** under Section 9(5): For certain services (currently passenger transport, restaurant, housekeeping via app), the ECO pays GST on the full value on behalf of unregistered suppliers. **Tour/guide services are not currently notified under 9(5)** — monitor for changes.

**Recommendation:** Model your business as **Principal @ 5% without ITC** under HSN 9985. It's the simplest and most defensible. Get a CA to confirm before your first invoice.

### SEIS — Discontinued, Don't Budget

[SEIS](https://www.dgft.gov.in/CP/?opt=ft-policy) is dead for services rendered after April 1, 2020. No service-side successor scheme has been announced. Zero impact on your plan.

### TDS on Guide Payouts

| Section | Rate | Threshold | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **194C (contractor)** ← **use this** | **1%** (individual/HUF) | ₹30K single / ₹1L aggregate p.a. | Guide treated as contractor |
| 194H (commission) | 2% (reduced from 5% Oct 2024) | ₹20K p.a. | If structured as commission to agent |
| 194O (e-commerce) | 1% | ₹5L p.a. | If platform classified as e-commerce operator |

**Use Section 194C @ 1%.** PAN collection at onboarding is mandatory — without PAN, rate jumps to 20% under Section 206AA. Make it a hard onboarding gate.

Monthly cycle: deposit TDS by 7th of next month, file Form 26Q quarterly, issue Form 16A to guides.

## 7. Student Guide Legal Status — Fewer Landmines Than You'd Think

### Can Indian College Students Earn This Way? Yes.

- **Indian Contract Act Section 11**: full contractual capacity at 18+.
- **UGC**: no regulation bars student earnings. Its 2020 "earn while you learn" guidelines under NEP 2020 actively encourage it.
- **State/central law**: no bar on gig/contractor earnings by students.
- **College/university rules**: many have "full-time student" clauses — but these are contracts between student and university, not enforceable against you. Worst case: student faces academic sanction. Zero liability to Mumbai Buddies.

**Protective clause in Guide Agreement:**
> *"Guide represents and warrants that they have all necessary permissions from their educational institution to undertake engagements via the Platform."*

Shifts any fallout to the guide. No active verification needed.

### Taxation of Student Guides

- **Basic exemption FY 2026-27 (new regime):** ₹3 lakh (possibly raised in Budget 2025 — verify).
- **Section 87A rebate:** zero tax up to ₹7L income under new regime.
- **Section 44AD presumptive taxation:** available for tour guide as "eligible business" — deemed profit 6% (digital receipts) / 8%. If guide earns ₹2L, deemed profit ₹12K, well below exemption.
- **GST threshold for services:** ₹20L aggregate turnover. Virtually no student guide will hit this individually.

A guide earning ₹15K/month has zero tax liability and doesn't need to file ITR (though filing to claim TDS refund is advisable).

### Hard Gates at Onboarding

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| **Indian citizen only** | Foreign students on visas CANNOT legally earn in India under MHA rules — violation triggers deportation. |
| **18+ only** | Under-18 contracts are **void ab initio** under Indian Contract Act Section 11 — unenforceable. Insurance issues. |
| **PAN mandatory** | Without PAN, TDS jumps from 1% to 20%. Non-negotiable. |
| **Aadhaar verified** | Age + citizenship verification in one step. |
| **Bank account** | For direct payouts. |
| **Background check** | See Liability section below. |

Bake these into the signup form as hard validations — don't try to handle edge cases post-hoc.

### Contractor vs Employee Classification Risk

The Supreme Court's test (*Balwant Rai Saluja v. AIR India*, 2014) looks at control, integration, economic reality, and mutuality of obligation. A guide reclassified as employee triggers EPF (12%+12%), ESI (if <₹21K/month), gratuity, bonus, POSH, maternity benefit, leave.

**The 10-point checklist to preserve contractor status:**

1. Guide chooses which tours to accept (mutuality broken)
2. Guide sets own hours and availability
3. Guide uses own phone, SIM, clothing — **no uniforms**
4. Written contractor agreement with explicit "independent contractor" clause
5. No exclusivity — guides can work for competitors
6. Payment per-tour, not monthly salary
7. Multiple guides served (you're a marketplace, not staffing agency)
8. No performance reviews, PIPs, or "termination" — just platform deactivation
9. TDS under 194C, Form 16A, not Form 16 (salary)
10. Public-facing copy uses "partner / Buddy / independent local" — never "hiring, employment, staff"

### Gig Worker Welfare Legislation — Not Yet in Maharashtra

As of April 2026, the following states have enacted gig worker welfare laws imposing transaction-based cess (1–5% typically) and registration requirements on platforms:

- **Rajasthan** (2023, operational)
- **Karnataka** (2024/2025, operational)
- **Jharkhand** ([August 2025, operational](https://shramadhan.jharkhand.gov.in/ftp/WebAdmin/documents/Gig-worker-social-security.pdf))
- **Bihar** (enacted)
- **Telangana** (draft)

**Maharashtra has NOT enacted one** as of April 2026. A draft bill circulated in 2024 but hasn't passed. Mumbai Buddies is not currently liable for any state cess.

The **Code on Social Security 2020** (central) defines "gig worker" and "platform worker" and would require ~1–2% aggregator contribution to a social security fund. **Not yet fully notified**. When it is, model this as a pass-through cost to travelers.

**Action:** Monitor Maharashtra Labour Department notifications quarterly. Add buffer in your financial model.

## 8. Liability Shielding Architecture

**The real shield is a *combination* — no single clause survives Indian courts. Build all four layers.**

### Layer 1: Terms of Service (User-facing)

Position as **technology intermediary** ("we connect; we don't provide"). Include:

- **Intermediary disclaimer** — Section 79 IT Act positioning.
- **Assumption of risk** — travel carries inherent risks.
- **Limitation of liability** — capped at greater of fees paid in last 12 months or ₹10,000. **Do NOT exclude liability for gross negligence or willful misconduct** — courts strike the whole clause down when you try.
- **No consequential damages** — exclude indirect, incidental, punitive, lost profits.
- **User indemnification of platform** — enforceable under Indian Contract Act Section 124.
- **Governing law + arbitration**: Indian law, Mumbai seat, MCIA institutional arbitration. **But explicitly preserve Consumer Protection Act jurisdiction** — *Emaar MGF v. Aftab Singh* (2019) means consumers can always approach consumer forums regardless of arbitration. Don't fight it; draft defensively.
- **Mandatory grievance redressal** as first step before litigation.
- **Refund/cancellation policy** (see §8.5 below).

**Indian enforceability caveats:**
- Caps shockingly low for the harm claimed → CPA 2019 can declare "unfair."
- Forum-selection ousting Indian consumer courts → void under Section 34 CPA.
- Complete liability disclaimers → won't survive "deficiency in service."

### Layer 2: Guide Contractor Agreement

Essential clauses:

**Contractor status preservation** (see §7 checklist).

**Indemnification of platform:**
> *"Guide shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the Company, its directors, officers, employees, and affiliates from and against any claims, damages, losses, liabilities, costs, and expenses (including reasonable legal fees) arising out of or relating to: (i) Guide's acts, omissions, or negligence during provision of Services; (ii) any breach by Guide of this Agreement or applicable law; (iii) any injury, illness, death, or property damage caused by Guide to Travelers or third parties; (iv) any tax or regulatory liability attributable to Guide."*

**IP ownership** — platform owns all content (photos, itineraries, reviews) via perpetual royalty-free license. Full assignment cleaner; license acceptable.

**Non-compete — India-specific trap:** Under **Section 27 Indian Contract Act 1872**, restraint of trade is **void**. Post-termination non-competes are **unenforceable** (*Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning*, 1967; *Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan*, 2006).

**What DOES work:**
- ✅ **Non-solicitation of customers** introduced via platform — enforceable if narrow and time-limited (6–12 months). *Wipro v. Beckman Coulter* (Delhi HC 2006).
- ✅ **Confidentiality/trade secret** — enforceable indefinitely.
- ✅ **Non-use of platform data** — enforceable (it's IP, not trade restraint).

**Do not** include "shall not work for competitors" — it sinks the whole clause.

**Mandatory clauses:**
- Background verification consent ([IDfy](https://www.idfy.com), [Signzy](https://signzy.com), AuthBridge) — ~₹200–500/check.
- Police character certificate refreshed annually.
- Insurance obligation (platform arranges group policy, deducts premium from payouts).
- Code of conduct with zero tolerance for harassment, theft, substance use.
- Arbitration seat Mumbai; governing law India.

### Layer 3: Intermediary Safe Harbor (IT Act Section 79)

**What it protects:** Third-party content (guide profiles, itineraries, reviews).

**What it does NOT protect:** Service-level misconduct (how the tour actually goes). You're liable on contract regardless.

**Due diligence requirements under IT Rules 2021 (amended 2022/2023):**

1. Publish ToS and Privacy Policy with prohibited content categories.
2. **Grievance Officer** — Indian resident, contact published. Acknowledge within 24 hours, resolve within 15 days.
3. Takedown within 36 hours of court/government order.
4. Preserve records 180 days post-removal.
5. Annual reminder to users of Rules.
6. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 overlay: nodal officer, grievance mechanism, 48-hour response to consumer complaints.

**Shreya Singhal v. UoI (2015)** — "actual knowledge" means court order / government notification, not private complaints. Safe harbor is fairly robust in India.

**But:** Curated marketplaces (which you are — you vet guides) face weakened shield per *Christian Louboutin v. Nakul Bajaj* (Delhi HC 2018) and *Amazon v. Amway* (2020). The more you control (pricing, KYC, payments, quality), the less you look like a neutral intermediary.

**Practical:** Don't over-rely on Section 79. Build the other three layers.

### Layer 4: Insurance (see §10)

### 8.5 Refund / Cancellation Policy

CPA 2019 and RBI PA Guidelines require a clear refund policy. Suggested tiers:

| Timing | Refund |
|---|---|
| >72 hrs before tour | 100% minus payment gateway charges |
| 24–72 hrs | 50% |
| <24 hrs | No refund; credit voucher valid 30 days |
| No-show by Buddy | 100% refund + ₹500 platform credit |
| Force majeure (flight cancel, medical — with proof) | 100% refund |

**EU 14-day cooling-off (Consumer Rights Directive):** Services for a **specific date** (tours) are exempt under Article 16(l). Explicitly cite this in ToS.

### 8.6 Women's Safety Protocol — Non-Negotiable

Given the Indian market's reputation risk, this is both the right thing to do and a strong commercial differentiator. Minimum stack:

1. **Background verification via IDfy or Signzy** at onboarding + annual police character certificate.
2. **In-app SOS** linked to 112 (national emergency) and your on-call ops team.
3. **Live trip tracking** shared with platform + traveler's emergency contact.
4. **Female traveler option** to request female Buddy (no extra cost; use demand signal to recruit more female Buddies).
5. **Mandatory guide safety/consent training** with signed acknowledgment.
6. **Written women's safety policy** published on website.
7. **ICC constituted under POSH Act** — mandatory if ≥10 workers (including contractors, arguably). Do it voluntarily regardless — it's a talking point with female travelers and investors.
8. **Incident escalation SOP** with <15 min response time and a named on-call founder.
9. **Insurance rider** for incident response costs.

## 9. DPDP Act 2023 + Rules 2025 — You Have a 2027 Runway

### Big Update

**The [DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on November 13, 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)** — the law is real, not draft. Phased implementation:

- **Phase 1 (Nov 13, 2025 — already effective):** Data Protection Board of India established; procedural framework live.
- **Phase 2 (Nov 13, 2026):** Consent Manager registration and obligations.
- **Phase 3 (May 13, 2027):** Substantive compliance — notice/consent standards, security safeguards, breach notification, retention, erasure, data principal rights.

**You have until May 13, 2027 for full substantive compliance.** Build toward it from Day 1 — it's cheaper than retrofitting.

### Your Obligations (as Data Fiduciary)

1. **Consent-based processing** — free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, affirmative action. In English + any of 22 scheduled languages on request. Never pre-ticked.
2. **Privacy notice** covering data categories, purposes, rights, DPB grievance mechanism, cross-border transfer details.
3. **Data Principal rights** — access, correction, erasure, grievance, nomination. Respond within timeline (final rules specify).
4. **Children's data (under 18)** — verifiable parental consent required. No tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted ads to minors.
5. **Grievance Officer** — Indian contact person published in app + website.
6. **Cross-border transfers** — Section 16 allows transfers except to countries blacklisted by Central Government (negative list; no list published yet). Permissive compared to GDPR.
7. **Breach notification** — DPB + affected principals, likely within 72 hours.
8. **DPO only mandatory if designated Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF)** — you won't be at launch.

### Penalties

Up to **₹250 crore per instance** for failure to safeguard data. ₹200 Cr for children's data violations. ₹150 Cr for SDF failures.

### Minimum-Viable Compliance Checklist (Do Now)

- [ ] Privacy Policy covering all DPDP notice requirements (bespoke lawyer review ₹15–40K; templates ₹3–8K)
- [ ] Consent flow with separate checkboxes: essential / marketing / analytics (never pre-ticked)
- [ ] Grievance Officer appointed, contact published on website + app
- [ ] Data inventory spreadsheet: what you collect, why, where stored (Supabase = AWS Mumbai region? confirm), retention
- [ ] Light-touch DPIA document
- [ ] One-page breach response runbook
- [ ] Vendor DPAs with Supabase, Razorpay, PostHog/Mixpanel
- [ ] 18+ age gate at signup (avoids children's data regime entirely)
- [ ] Right-to-erasure endpoint (`/delete-account` in settings)
- [ ] Cookie banner on marketing site (for EU visitors, GDPR overlap)

### GDPR Overlap — Watch Your Marketing

If you **market to EU residents** (EU-language site, EUR pricing, targeted EU ads), **GDPR applies extraterritorially regardless of DPDP**. Mumbai Buddies targeting international layover travelers plausibly triggers this. Mitigate by:
- Avoiding EU-language-specific landing pages at launch
- Cookie banner with proper consent mechanics
- Privacy policy mentioning GDPR rights for EU users

## 11. Peer Company Teardown — How They Structure It

### Airbnb Experiences
- **Entity:** Airbnb India Pvt Ltd
- **Structure:** Marketplace intermediary under IT Act. Hosts = independent contractors.
- **TDS:** Under Section 194-O (e-commerce operator) @ 1%.
- **MoT approval:** No.
- **Guide licensing:** Self-certification; Airbnb doesn't verify. Experiences avoid guiding inside ASI premises.
- **Language:** "Experiences by hosts, not tours." Aggressive intermediary framing.

### Klook (Singapore HQ) / GetYourGuide (Berlin) / Viator (US)
- **Structure:** OTA/marketplace listing local operators (not individual guides).
- **Operator** on the ground holds any guide license/permit.
- **MoT approval:** No.
- **Supplier Agreement** contractually pushes ALL license/permit/insurance responsibility to the operator.
- **Learning:** Three major OTAs all use the same supplier-carries-license shield.

### ToursByLocals (Canada)
- **Closest analog to Mumbai Buddies.** Marketplace for individual independent guides globally.
- Vets guides' backgrounds; transfers licensing compliance to guide.
- Uses "local guide / companion" language.
- Not MoT-approved.
- **Learning:** Scalable, operates in India, same legal shield stack.

### Withlocals (Netherlands)
- **Tagline:** "Meet a local, not a tour guide."
- **Learning:** Deliberate language choice to sidestep guide-licensing regimes globally. Copy this playbook.

### Reality Tours & Travel Mumbai (Contrast — NOT a marketplace)
- **Entity:** Pvt Ltd, principal tour operator
- **MoT approval:** Yes
- Employs/contracts guides directly; has in-house training; some hold RLG
- Full GST registration; 80% profits reportedly to community NGO (Reality Gives)
- **Learning:** They accept full operator liability because they ARE the operator. Mumbai Buddies should explicitly NOT be this.

### Urban Company (non-tourism marketplace precedent)
- **Structure:** Two-sided marketplace for home services
- **Regulatory pressure:** Labor classification scrutiny (2022–23 Karnataka); Code on Social Security 2020 "gig worker" definition
- **Learning:** Even "marketplaces" face labor classification pressure. Keep student guides genuinely independent.

### GoWithGuide (formerly Triplelights)
- Japanese-origin marketplace for local guides globally; operates India listings
- Marketplace model; commission-taking; guide carries license
- Not MoT-approved
- **Learning:** Same playbook.

## 13. Red Flags & Things to Monitor

### Regulatory Items Moving in 2026–27

1. **Central Code on Social Security 2020** — full notification triggers ~1–2% aggregator cess on your turnover. Watch Ministry of Labour notifications.
2. **Maharashtra gig worker welfare bill** — reportedly drafted; not yet enacted. Monitor Maharashtra Labour Dept.
3. **GST Council Section 9(5) notifications** — if "local tour/guide services" is added, GST treatment shifts to you-pay-on-full-value.
4. **DPDP Rules phase 2 (Nov 2026) + phase 3 (May 2027)** — substantive obligations activate.
5. **PA-CB net worth floor rising to ₹25 Cr by March 2026** — doesn't affect you (you're using a licensed PA-CB), but confirms consolidation in the payment layer.

### Operational Things That Could Bite You

1. **Hiring staff as employees triggers EPF/ESI/gratuity/POSH compliance** — these kick in at 10 employees and 20 employees thresholds respectively. Avoid the threshold by using contractors where appropriate (still genuine contractors per §7 checklist).
2. **Crossing ₹20L turnover triggers GST registration** — plan for it. Don't straddle ambiguously.
3. **EU marketing triggers GDPR** — be deliberate about geo-targeting.
4. **Curating guides too tightly weakens intermediary shield** — balance between trust (vetting good) and control (vetting bad for shield).
5. **Single safety incident can end the brand** — spend disproportionately on verification, live tracking, and incident response. It's cheap insurance.
6. **Director criminal exposure is narrow** (Maksud Saiyed doctrine) but **POSH non-compliance, CPA false advertising, and IT Act 85 violations** can attach personally. Constitute ICC, maintain compliance records, avoid marketing claims you can't back up.

### Things That Look Like Loopholes But Aren't

1. **"We earn forex so it's export" under GST** — NO. Place of supply rules defeat this for inbound tours.
2. **"Students are volunteers, not workers"** — NO. If money changes hands, it's an engagement; classification rules apply.
3. **"IT Act Section 79 shields everything"** — NO. It shields content, not service conduct.
4. **"We're just a website, not a tour operator"** — weak argument when you take commission, vet guides, and handle payments. Courts see through it.

## 15. Final Cautions

- **None of this is legal advice.** Get a one-time paid consult (₹15–25K) before committing capital or signing agreements — the cost is trivial compared to mistakes.
- Regulation is moving fast in 2025–26 (DPDP Rules rollout, Code on Social Security notification pending, Maharashtra gig bill, GST Council clarifications). Assign someone to monitor monthly.
- The single biggest mistake bootstrappers make here is **trying to claim GST export on inbound tours**. Don't. 5% tour operator rate with no ITC is correct and simpler.
- The second biggest is **not reading ToS/Privacy/Contractor templates carefully** and missing Indian-specific carve-outs (Section 27 restraint of trade, CPA non-ouster of jurisdiction, POSH ICC). Bespoke review is worth it.
- The third biggest is **skipping insurance** because it feels expensive when tours/year is low. One incident undoes everything.

Your market opening is real — no student-guide marketplace exists for CSMIA layovers, the regulatory environment is permissive, and Indian infrastructure (payments, background verification, incorporation) has become world-class in the last 5 years. The compliance lift is meaningful but very manageable with the playbook above.
